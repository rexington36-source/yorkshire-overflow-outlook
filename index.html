Yorkshire Overflow Outlook

The modelled chance that each Yorkshire Water storm overflow starts discharging in the next 6, 12 or 24 hours, refreshed hourly from weather forecasts and the live spill feed.

Sorted by chance of a discharge starting

    How to read this

    Chance a discharge starts is the model's probability that a new discharge begins within the window, after at least 12 hours without one. Bands: unlikely under 5%, possible 5–25%, likely over 25%. It is a probability, not a warning.

    No probability is shown while an overflow is discharging, for 12 hours after a discharge ends, or when its monitor is offline. Dry-weather check counts discharges that began with no rain in the previous 48 hours by both forecast-model rainfall and Environment Agency rain gauges, with no discharge in the day before. A gauge check counts only if a rain gauge within 15 km has data for at least 90% of those 48 hours; from 30 October 2026 the counts were revised on that basis (previously any nearby gauge counted, even one with no data for the period).

    The model uses ECMWF forecast and recent rainfall (via Open-Meteo), each overflow's recent discharge history, and the Environment Agency's Event Duration Monitoring records. Every forecast is stored and later checked against what happened.

    Modelled estimates, not measurements.

    Storm overflows discharging in heavy rain are permitted under environmental permits and are not in themselves unlawful.

    The underlying Event Duration Monitoring data has not been verified by the Environment Agency.

    A dry-weather flag is an anomaly worth investigating, not an accusation.
